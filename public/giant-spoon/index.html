01 / Delivery model

From first brief to last file.

A production route Giant Spoon can put in front of Creative and the client: what we are making, who owns the next decision, and what it costs.

01 · Intake Frame the job

Deliverables, medium, decision-makers, constraints, and the first budget envelope.

02 · Route Build the board

Director and vendor options, bid comparison, schedule, and the assumptions Creative needs to hear early.

03 · Review gates Hold the line

Concept, treatment, offline, finish, and legal/talent checks have named owners and dates.

04 · Produce Run concurrent work

Crew, vendors, and client notes stay on one board while other jobs keep moving.

05 · Deliver Close the job

Specs, versions, archive, and cost reconciliation — then a clean handoff to media and account.

06 · Next brief Keep the roster

Vendor notes and what the last job taught us go back into the next estimate.

03 / Operating workflow

Intake. Routing. Review gates.

The operating map I wrote and used at Final Frontier so ownership, approvals, and handoffs are visible — then applied it to 6–8 live campaigns at peak.

Production operating system

One board, named owners

Intake captures the brief and constraints. Routing assigns Creative, production, and vendor owners. Review gates stop work from skipping concept, treatment, offline, or legal. The map is a working tool, not a slide for the wall.

What this is not Final Frontier’s later Los Angeles studio expansion is not my work. $8M+ is the aggregate of Final Frontier plus earlier brand work, not a single campaign.
